# The Annual Operating Calendar: A Year in the Life of a Shopping Center

Every shopping center runs the same annual loop: budgets, reconciliations, renewals, inspections, storm seasons, holidays, and year-end close. Owners who know the loop experience it as a rhythm; owners who do not experience it as a series of ambushes — the reconciliation deadline that appears from nowhere, the insurance renewal negotiated in a hurry, the holiday parking plan improvised in November. This article maps the operating year for a multi-tenant retail center, month by month, and explains how to turn the map into a calendar your operation actually runs on. Use the interactive explorer below to walk the year.

## Why the year has a shape

Three forces shape the calendar. **Finance** sets the winter rhythm: the prior year's CAM reconciliation, the new budget, insurance and tax milestones. **Weather** sets the seasonal rhythm: in Gulf South markets, storm preparation dominates late spring and early summer, while exterior work clusters in the mild months. **Retail** sets the fall rhythm: back-to-school leasing, holiday traffic planning, and January's post-holiday reset. Most missed deadlines in property management are not knowledge failures; they are calendar failures — the task was known, but nothing surfaced it at the right moment.

## Working the calendar

The calendar only works as a system if three things are true. First, every item above that involves a lease obligation — reconciliation deadlines, insurance requirements, notice windows — must be verified against your actual lease language, because lease terms control over any generic calendar. Second, each monthly block needs a named owner and a defined artifact: not "check the roof" but "file the spring roof inspection memo in the safe." Third, the calendar should live where the reminders live. If your system fires alerts from the same records that hold the documents, the calendar maintains itself; if it lives on paper, someone must carry it deliberately.

### Regional adjustment

This calendar reflects a Gulf South rhythm — storm season in June, mild winters, exterior work in shoulder seasons. Other regions transpose it: freeze-market centers front-load winterization and push exterior work into summer; desert markets plan around heat rather than storms. The structure — finance in winter, maintenance by season, retail by the holiday clock — survives the move even when the months do not.

## The view from Arnould Blvd

On The Blvd Shopping Center is a roughly 63,000 SF legacy center in Lafayette, and its year is the Gulf South version of the calendar above: reconciliation and budget season through winter, exterior and site work in the mild months, storm preparation complete before June, and the holiday rhythm anchored by the center's tenants. A legacy asset adds one more annual item worth naming: the walk-through audit. Once a year, walk every suite line, every roof section, every parking aisle, and every sign face with the checklist from Article 36 — because on an older property, the year always has one surprise, and the calendar's job is to make sure it is a small one.

## The practical next step

1. Copy the twelve monthly blocks above into your own system, with owners and dates.
2. Verify every lease-driven date against actual lease language this month — especially reconciliation and insurance deadlines.
3. Schedule the recurring inspections (roof, HVAC, fire-safety, signage) for the full year now, while contractors still have open calendars.
4. Set the storm-prep or winterization block 90 days ahead of your region's season, not 30.
5. Put the year-end close on the calendar today: December's work is won or lost in October's preparation.
