# The Complete Property Management Checklist: Every Recurring Task, Organized by Rhythm

A shopping center is a machine with a few hundred moving parts, and most of those parts are on a schedule. The rent cycle runs monthly. Insurance certificates expire on scattered dates. Roof inspections belong to specific seasons. Budgets, reconciliations, and tax appeals each have their own calendar. The owners who sleep well are rarely the ones with the best memory — they are the ones whose operation runs on a checklist. This article publishes the master checklist this series has been building toward: every recurring task in operating a multi-tenant retail center, organized by rhythm, in a form you can work through, save, and print.

## How to use this checklist

The checklist below is interactive. Checking items saves your progress in this browser, so you can work through it over multiple sessions. The sections follow the natural rhythm of a center — daily and weekly field work, monthly financial work, quarterly compliance and vendor work, and annual planning work — plus the standing records every operation should maintain. Three habits make a checklist like this actually run a property:

1. **Assign an owner to each section.** A checklist nobody owns becomes decoration. Each section should have one name next to it.
2. **Work the rhythm, not the mood.** The daily and weekly items happen on schedule whether or not anything feels urgent. Most expensive property problems began as a weekly item that kept getting skipped.
3. **Review monthly, revise quarterly.** The checklist is a living document. When something is missed, the response is to add or clarify an item — not to assign blame.

### A note on scope

Every center is different — size, age, region, lease structure. Treat this as a strong default, then edit: add the tasks your leases require, remove what does not apply, and adjust frequencies to your market and your property's condition. A Gulf South center carries storm season; a snow-market center carries winterization. The structure travels; the specifics should not.

## The master checklist

Work through the sections in order, or jump to the rhythm that matches where you are in the month. Your progress is saved automatically.

## The view from Arnold Blvd

On The Blvd Shopping Center is a roughly 70,000 SF legacy multi-tenant center in Lafayette — and it is exactly the kind of property this checklist was built around. Legacy assets generate more maintenance events than new construction, and multi-tenant means every lease adds its own obligations to the rhythm. Under the Operator Landlord approach, the checklist is also a tenant-relations tool: the center whose common areas are inspected weekly, whose certificates are current, and whose work orders close on schedule is a center tenants renew in.

## The practical next step

1. Open the checklist and check off, honestly, what your operation already does reliably. That is your baseline.
2. Assign a named owner to every section — including the ones you own personally.
3. Pick the three highest-value unchecked items and put them on this month's calendar with dates.
4. Print the checklist for the field binder, and keep the digital copy as the system of record.
5. Revisit quarterly: add what you missed, remove what does not serve the property, and keep the rhythm.
