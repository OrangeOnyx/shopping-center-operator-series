# Buying a neighborhood strip: the diligence stack

The listing package will lead with occupancy and NOI. That is the seller’s story. Your stack should be: survey and plat, parking variance, access and easements, leases with exhibits, then the rent roll as a control document that has to reconcile to all of the above.

If you start with NOI you will underwrite a machine you have not seen. Neighborhood strips hide their risk in the plat: reciprocal drives, liquor lines, remote lots, sold-out notches, church waivers that survive termination.

## What to pull before you fly

Recorded plat (here: Montagnet & Domingue, 1994, last revised 2019). Variance entry number. Assessor parcels — all of them, including overflow. Reciprocal servitude instruments. Anchor HVAC and exclusive-use riders. Meter maps. You are not collecting trivia. You are mapping what you cannot change after closing.

## A working rule

Do not “correct” proper nouns. On this case the street is Arnould and the subdivision of record is Arnold Heights Subd. Ext. No. 1. They look like a typo. They are not. Buyers who tidy the names in their model invent a different property.
