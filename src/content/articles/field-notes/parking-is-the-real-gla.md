# Parking is the real GLA

Owners talk GLA. Cities talk stalls. The tighter of the two is the real capacity of the center. On The Boulevard, zoning wanted 344 spaces. A recorded variance allows 324 provided, and it says the quiet part out loud: floor space is limited to available parking. That sentence belongs on the first page of every lease file, every LOI, and every conversation about converting a service suite into a restaurant.

## Two numbers, two jobs

Cite 324 when you are talking to zoning, lenders, and buyers. Plan operations on what the plat actually stripes — here, 314, with a ten-stall gap that may live on an unlabeled Johnston row still waiting on ground confirmation. Mixing those numbers is how you approve a use the field cannot absorb.

Also watch green area. A 20 percent landscape requirement is not decoration. Removing a stall row to “open the front” can violate both parking and green at once.

## Leasing against the cap

High-turnover uses (quick service, clinics with long dwell, fitness) eat stalls. Before you celebrate a LOI, run the use against the remaining stall budget, not against empty SF. A vacant 2,000 SF box is not automatically leasable to the highest rent if it consumes the last restaurant-capable parking.
