# Underwriting an open-air center

A neighborhood strip is not a black-box cap-rate. The underwriting sequence is: legal capacity (parking, zoning, exclusives), contractual cash (leases as total rent, not base), recoveries (CAM, tax, insurance), then vacancy that is actually leasable under those constraints.

If you cap-rate a trailing twelve and call it done, you will pay for restaurant occupancy the stall field cannot support, or for a “vacant” suite that is owner-occupied and not on the market.

## Stress the calendar, not just occupancy

Three or four expirations in the same year is a financing event, not a footnote. So is an unresolved commencement date. A model that assumes every blank end-date is a holdover is already wrong. Some blanks mean the file is incomplete — treat them as risk, not as income continuity.

## Recoveries are not a rounding error

Present tenant rent as total (base + additional) unless you are explicitly showing a PSF stack. Lenders and owners who live in “base only” will misread every comparable in a tertiary market.
