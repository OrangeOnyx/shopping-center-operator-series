# Easements That Outlive Their Contracts: Church Waivers, Bank Stalls, and the Parcel You Don't Own

A rent roll tells you who pays. The recorded instruments tell you what the land may be used for, who else may use it, and which rights survive the deals that created them. At a legacy center the instrument list runs long: utility easements, access servitudes with neighbors, parking agreements, use restrictions carried in from an adjoining owner, and the occasional corner parcel that was sold off years ago and now sits inside the center's outline like a missing tooth. Each one changes what a buyer is buying and what an operator may lease. This article walks three kinds of instruments that show up at neighborhood centers, using the real ones at On The Blvd as the teaching case, and ends with the reading discipline that keeps them from surprising you.

## The waiver that survives termination

Neighbors with a legal say over your property are common: a church, a school, or a residential subdivision may hold rights, by statute or by contract, to restrict certain uses within a stated distance. Alcohol sales are the classic example. A restaurant prospect at a center near a church may be barred from a liquor license by a distance rule unless the church has waived it.

At On The Blvd, the waiver lives inside an access-and-parking easement with the neighboring church, granted for a 25-year term. Section 3a of that easement waives the church's objection to alcohol sales within the protected distance, and the waiver is written to **survive termination** of the easement itself. The plat draws the resulting liquor line: an arc struck from the church parcel's corner across the side street, roughly 175 feet in radius, that enters the short building's storefront, crosses one demising wall, and exits the rear. Suites on one side of the line may sell alcohol; suites on the other may not, and one suite straddles it.

Three lessons for the operator:

- **Read for survival clauses.** An easement's term is one thing; the rights that outlive it are another. Survival language is where the durable value hides.
- **Draw the line.** A restriction that exists only in a paragraph gets forgotten. A restriction drawn on the site plan is consulted every time a restaurant prospect calls.
- **Match the waiver to the leasing plan.** The suites on the permitted side of the line are the center's restaurant inventory. That fact belongs in the tenant-mix strategy, not in a title file.

## The neighbor's stalls and the shared driveway

Reciprocal easements are agreements between adjoining owners to share access, parking, or both. They are indispensable at corners where one parcel would otherwise be landlocked, and they are also where operators most often misstate what they own.

At On The Blvd the corner parcel at the highway intersection was sold and is occupied by a bank. It is not part of the center. The recorded plat shows it as a notch in the center's outline, labeled "NOT A PART," and the reciprocal access-and-parking servitude that governs the relationship is recorded as Entry 2004-00057697. Under its operating terms the center counts 13 spaces on the bank parcel toward its parking supply, the two owners share a driveway on the boulevard and the drive-aisle that runs behind the bank to the highway, maintenance of the shared areas is split, and the servitude expires on December 30, 2034.

That expiry is the whole point. Thirteen spaces are part of the count the center's parking variance depends on. When the servitude ends, so does the right to count them, and any lease that assumed them is exposed. The date belongs on the critical-dates board today, years ahead, with the parking reconciliation re-run well before it arrives.

One caution the operator has recorded in the property file: the site plan labels the shared driveway and aisles as shared based on the plat's arrows, not on the servitude's own exhibit, which has not yet been pulled from the recorder. Until that exhibit is in hand, the plan does not assert the servitude's legal boundary. That is the right discipline. A drawn line implies certainty; certainty comes from the recorded exhibit.

### What a reciprocal easement file should contain

- The recorded instrument by entry number, with every amendment and supersession noted.
- The exhibit that describes the servitude area, drawn onto your site plan from the exhibit, not from memory.
- The operating terms: who maintains what, in what shares, and who insures the shared area.
- The count of any parking spaces you rely on, and where they appear in your parking reconciliation.
- The expiry or renewal mechanics, calendared with a notice window long enough to renegotiate.

## The parcel you do not own, drawn as if you did

The most common error on a marketing site plan is drawing the whole block as the center. An illustrator working from an aerial sees a continuous parking lot and a building at the corner and draws one property. The plat says otherwise. At On The Blvd, a third-party rendering commissioned for marketing was graded against the recorded plat and failed on exactly this point: no boundary, the bank parcel drawn as center property, the median openings and islands wrong, and stall counts that could not be trusted.

The rule that follows is simple. Every drawing of the center — flyer, offering memorandum, tenant exhibit, insurance application — is traced from the recorded plat and shows the "NOT A PART" notch. A buyer who receives a site plan that includes land the seller does not own has a diligence finding before the first meeting. A tenant whose exhibit shows parking the landlord cannot deliver has an argument at the first dispute.

## Reading the instrument list

Every recorded plat carries an exceptions list: the instruments the surveyor found of record that affect the land. It is the operator's reading assignment. For each entry, the file should answer four questions: what right does it grant or restrict, who holds it, where on the ground does it apply, and when does it end. Utility easements are usually permanent and rarely matter until someone wants to build on them; a 10-foot utility strip or a guy-wire easement at a pole can block a monument sign or a patio. Temporary easements expire; the plat may still show one that has lapsed, and the file should say so. Access servitudes and parking agreements have terms and money attached. Use restrictions may run with the land indefinitely or may end with the agreement that created them, and the difference is decided by a survival clause.

Two habits make the list manageable. First, the instrument facts live in one structured record — the same record that feeds the site plan, the compliance matrix, and the critical-dates board — so a date changed in the file moves itself everywhere it is displayed. Second, every "not yet pulled" exhibit is logged as an open item with a name and a date, because an easement the operator has not read is an easement the operator does not understand.

### The view from Arnould Blvd

On The Blvd Shopping Center, roughly 63,000 SF at 101–149 Arnould Blvd in Lafayette, carries all three instruments described here on its plat: the church easement whose liquor waiver survives termination, the reciprocal servitude with the sold corner parcel and its 2034 expiry, and the utility and guy easements that constrain where a sign or a patio may go. Under the Operator Landlord approach they are not title-file trivia. The liquor line is drawn on the interactive site plan and switched off only for a clean leasing exhibit; the bank parcel renders as a notch with its own label; the servitude's expiry sits on the critical-dates board next to lease expirations; and the missing exhibit is an open item with a name on it. The instruments outlived the deals that made them. The operating system makes sure they do not outlive the operator's attention.

## The practical next step

1. Copy the exceptions list from your recorded plat and give every entry a one-line answer: right, holder, location, end date.
2. Find every survival clause in your easements and note which rights persist after termination.
3. Draw every use restriction and every "not a part" parcel onto the site plan you actually hand to prospects and buyers.
4. Calendar the expiry of any instrument whose parking or access your leasing depends on, with a notice window measured in years.
5. List the exhibits you have never read, and pull them this quarter.
